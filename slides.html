Quorum

Busy calendars, strong opinions, one trip everyone loves.

Kelly Hodge · MGT 409 · three agents on gpt-6-luna via Portkey
Not a trip planner — a way for a group to make a decision it will stick to.

Code: https://github.com/hodgeke10/Quorum  ·  Public repository.

The problem

Five friends, five calendars,
and a thread that goes quiet.

Availability, money and taste all conflict at once, and no one person can hold it in their head.

So the thread dies, or the loudest person decides and someone quietly resents it.

14 weeks in a year work for five people, out of 359 candidate start dates — for ten people it is 4
$1,150–$3,000 spread in what people can spend
12 sample travelers with real-shaped calendars; a group is drawn at random from them

The agents

Three roles. Two things deliberately not agents.

Scout — 1 run, with tools.
Builds a slate of 3–5 genuinely different priced options.
Advocate × N — 1 run per traveler, parallel, no tools.
Scores how well every option fits one person, 0–10.
Chair — 1 run, with tools.
Makes the call and writes who flexed for the group, and by how much.
Pricing is Python. A model can't invent a number it never had to add up.
The ranking is Python. The fairness rule is computed, not generated. The Chair explains it.

All three on gpt-6-luna, reasoning_effort=none (required for tool calls). Tools reach the data through one MCP server: trip-ops, 10 tools.

The idea, in one picture

Rows are people, columns are trips, cells are fit (10 = my dream trip, 0 = not for me). We pick the column with the highest floor — not the best average. New Orleans is $55 cheaper with a better average; its floor is a 4, so Austin wins.

The output

Denver, May 4–10 — mountain views, outdoor time, and city food and nightlife in mild 75°F weather.

Not just a destination — a ledger of who flexed, measured as the gap between how well this trip fits each person and how well their own favourite would have.

One batched survey tool replaced 19 one-at-a-time lookups. Every tool result is re-sent on every later request, so tool calls bill quadratically.

$1,148 per month at 10,000 users (2 runs each)
1.1¢ per person per trip decided

The tradeoff: slates got slightly less exotic — the survey's cheapest-first ordering buries the expensive-but-perfect option. The floor didn't drop.

All five advocates together are only 45% of tokens — the per-person part is the cheap part.

Safety

A calendar is a file someone else wrote.

The defense is structural. Event text never reaches an agent — availability travels as dates and names only. There is no tool that returns a calendar title, so there is no channel.

Measuring reach, not just effect, found two real holes: the orchestration once passed the payload to the Chair in a prompt, and the quarantine log was exposed as a tool the Scout could call. Both read clean on "did the attack work?" In an earlier build the subtle payload did steer the slate 3/3 — whether it works shifts with the model and the catalogue. Reach is the only property that holds by construction.

Limits & next

What I'd do next.

Limits

  • Fares are a snapshot, not live quotes
  • 111 destinations, not the world
  • One shared booking, split evenly
  • It decides a trip; it doesn't plan one

Next

  • Run it with a real group — everything else is speculation until the ledger is put in front of the person it says paid most
  • Let people challenge their own score and re-run — turn a recommendation into a negotiation
  • Real fare data: the schema is already right
  • Re-run when calendars change, and say what moved
